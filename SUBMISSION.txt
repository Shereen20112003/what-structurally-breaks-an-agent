EXPERIMENTS
The primary local study has 49,152 task instances and 393,216 controller
episodes. The follow-on local study has 1,156 configurations and 295,936
episodes with seeds disjoint from the primary study. Total: 689,152
programmed-reference-controller episodes. The test suite contains 35 tests.

An exploratory model-in-the-loop pilot adds 24 unique analyzed episodes, with one
Copilot Auto-routed GPT-6 Luna model, four two-level structural interventions,
and three task seeds per cell. Sixteen episodes use the original two seeds;
eight use a third seed added after inspecting the initial outcomes. The raw
log contains 29 records, including five repeated/recovery records excluded
from summaries. This is exploratory. A separate local CPU study uses one
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct quantized model in a complete 2^5 factorial: 256
planned episodes, eight paired seeds, and five task factors. Its smoke run
is stored separately and excluded. The protocol is local rather than
externally preregistered; the model study is single-model and does not
compare reasoning budgets. Neither model study supports cross-provider or
real-world claims. No learned controller, human participant, or external
validity result is included. The CLI usage record for the earlier Copilot
pilot reports 168 premium-request units and 16.180 billion nano-AIU including
its smoke test. The user later authorized a budget-limited Copilot
fractional-factorial experiment, accepting that the dollar equivalent and
account-wide remaining quota cannot be verified. Across those usage reports,
the logged total is 264 premium-request units and 25.653 billion nano-AIU;
these are not dollar amounts.

The completed local-model factorial yielded 0/256 completed workflows and
21/2,048 completed operations. Its zero marginal success contrasts are
uninformative because every condition is at the success floor. This is a
failed calibration for a useful model capability profile, not evidence that
the task factors have no effect. It does not repair the lack of cross-model
evidence or establish the paper's novelty.

A separate, predesigned Copilot fractional factorial completed 16/16
episodes in eight conditions over two task seeds. The routed GPT-6 Luna
model succeeded on 8/16 episodes and completed 88/128 operations. The
descriptive success contrasts were depth -25 pp, branching +50 pp, width
+25 pp, and snapshot feedback +50 pp. Because the design is a half fraction,
main effects may be aliased with three-factor interactions; only two task
seeds were used. The final planned episode was completed after inspecting
the preceding 15 outcomes during a pause, without changing the frozen
condition or design. These data remain exploratory, are not a full-factorial
study, and do not establish stable structural effects or a model profile.

NEAREST-WORK COMPARISON (TARGETED, NOT SYSTEMATIC)
DyVal / Illusion of Thinking:
  Direct overlap in controllable task complexity and model evaluation;
  reasoning/algorithm or puzzle settings, not this fixed-work tool protocol.
TRAJECT-Bench:
  Direct overlap in executable tool trajectories, depth/breadth controls,
  and step-level diagnostics. Current work lacks its realistic tool diversity.
Reward Hacking Benchmark:
  Direct overlap in chained tool tasks, harder variants, and multi-model
  experiments; its outcome is exploit propensity, not general capability.
SynthAPI:
  Direct overlap in controllable, executable, stateful tool environments;
  it studies training-data composition and generalization at far greater
  scale, not test-time diagnosis.
Synthetic POMDPs / Memory Demand Structure:
  Direct overlap in tunable memory difficulty with a formal demand model;
  it evaluates memory-augmented RL under partial observability. This paper's
  snapshot/history condition retains recoverable information and is not
  equivalent to partial observability.
MemCalib:
  Direct overlap in model memory evaluation; it tests influence of retrieved
  propositions on responses, not action-level register retention.
MCPAgentBench:
  Overlap in multi-step tool invocation, candidate lists, and distractors;
  its environment is based on MCP tool definitions and real-world tasks.

No claim of a wholly unique paper or "first benchmark" is made. Controllable
task difficulty, reasoning-budget studies, tool-trajectory diagnosis,
multi-step tool benchmarks, and memory benchmarks predate this work.
TRAJECT-Bench and the Reward Hacking Benchmark make the earlier broad
novelty framing untenable; SynthAPI adds a very recent, large-scale,
controllable executable-environment study. Synthetic POMDPs for memory-
demand structure provide a close precedent for tunable memory difficulty;
unlike that work, the current snapshot/history condition preserves
recoverable information and is not partial observability. The only candidate
distinction currently identifiable is a fixed-work, state-mutating tool
workflow with explicit audits of controller representation. Whether this
distinction is novel or useful requires systematic literature review and
direct benchmark comparison. The literature search is targeted, not
systematic; no novelty proof or direct benchmark comparison exists. The
24-episode Copilot pilot, 16-episode two-seed half-fraction, and 256-episode
single-model local factorial do not establish a general model-profile
contribution. Peer review may require substantial reframing
or a different contribution; the present PDF is not ready to call
camera-ready or fully evidenced.
