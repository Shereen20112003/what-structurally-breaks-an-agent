Anaggregatetaskscoredoesnotdistinguishfailuresofscheduling,

1 INTRODUCTION
What makes a task difficult for an agent? A long sequence may
require little planning if each next action is obvious. A short task
mayfailbecauseanecessaryvalueisnolongervisible.Manyavail-
able actions need not be problematic when their preconditions are
easy to evaluate. These cases have different remedies, yet a single
completion score treats them alike.
Existing work already establishes that task complexity matters.
DyValgeneratesreasoningproblemswithcontrollablegraphstruc-
ture[1];controlledpuzzlesexamineperformanceascompositional
complexity increases [2]; and task depth and capability breadth
have been studied in multi-agent evaluation [15]. More directly,
TRAJECT-Bench varies tool-trajectory breadth and depth and re-
ports trajectory-level selection, argument, and ordering metrics
[10]. The Reward Hacking Benchmark studies independent and
chained multi-step tool tasks across frontier models, including
harder task variants [11]. SynthAPI varies tool, task, and database
diversity in synthetic executable environments, although its ques-
tion is training-data scaling rather than capability diagnosis [12].
Synthetic POMDPs have also been designed with explicit, theoret-
ically characterized memory-demand structures while preserving
transition invariants [14]. Our remaining question is narrower:
can a small tool-execution workflow isolate state-retention and
feedback conditions while preserving required work and auditing
unavoidable structural couplings?
The scope of novelty is necessarily narrow. Controllable graph
difficulty, reasoning-budget comparisons, tool-trajectory diag-
nostics, multi-step tool tasks, and memory benchmarks all have
substantial prior work [1, 2, 4–7, 9–13]. Our candidate distinc-
tion is the combination of a fixed-work, state-mutating work-
flow with explicit contrasts between state snapshots and history-
reconstructible feedback, plus audits of how controller represen-
tation changes the resulting profile. This is a testable distinction,
not a novelty finding: neither the reference controllers nor the
two small, single-model evaluations validate a general capability-
profile contribution, and no direct benchmark comparison has
been performed.
We study four operational controls: the longest prerequisite
chain, the number of candidates within an operation, the number
of active state registers, and whether current register values are
repeated in feedback. The total number of required operations is
alwayseight.Thisisa fixed-work depth intervention:depthchanges
precedence, not episode length. Candidate count is likewise local
toanoperation,notthenumberofgloballyavailableactions.These
distinctions are necessary for interpreting the experiment.
The present study is a methods and calibration paper. All evalu-
ated controllers are short, inspectable programs with deliberately
restricted resources. Their failures are not surprising discoveries
about general reasoning. Instead, they are positive and negative
controls: an instrument that cannot distinguish a known retention
deficit from a known scheduling deficit should not be used to di-
agnose less interpretable systems. We now also report exploratory
evidence from one small local model, a small Copilot-routed pilot,
and a two-seed Copilot fractional factorial. None supports general
capability claims.
Our contributions are an executable fixed-work factorial task
family; an exact audit showing which structural metrics remain

fixed and which necessarily move together; and a reference-

the assumptions underlying those profiles. We also report one

The distinction between task structure and controller represen-
tation is especially important. Increasing the number of candidate
slots makes serial examination expensive, but not an indexed
lookup. Increasing the number of registers creates more values to
retain, but a known deterministic workflow can be compiled into
a list of future answers. Conversely, newly sampled unobserved
values cannot be recovered by a better retention policy. A use-
ful structural profile must say which of these cases its controls
measure.
2 RELATED WORK
Controlled complexity, memory demand, and tool trajectories.Dy-
Val uses directed acyclic graphs to generate reasoning tasks at ad-
justable complexities [1]. Shojaee et al. use controlled puzzle en-
vironments to study complexity-dependent performance and com-
putation, including reasoning effort under fixed inference budgets
[2]. MorphoBench adapts question difficulty to model capability
and evaluates GPT and Gemini models [3]. These works establish
that controllable difficulty and model-based scaling studies are not
new contributions by themselves. TRAJECT-Bench varies paral-
lel breadth and sequential depth in executable, production-style
tool trajectories and analyzes tool selection, argument correctness,
and ordering [10]. RHB evaluates chained tool workflows across
13 models and tests harder variants and environmental interven-
tions [11]. SynthAPI uses executable stateful environments and
independently controls tool, task, and database diversity to study
training-data composition across hundreds of thousands of tasks
[12]. Theirobjectivesdiffer fromours, buttheir scope makes broad
claims about the first controlled or structural tool-use benchmark
untenable. Wang et al. provide an especially relevant precedent
for controllable memory difficulty: their synthetic POMDPs define
memory-demand structures and construct tunable environments
while preserving the belief MDP [14]. Our snapshot/history con-
trast is not a partial-observability intervention, because success-
ful action/update history is sufficient to reconstruct the current
registers. It measures the burden of maintaining state internally
rather than a loss of information. The hidden-resampling exten-
sion introduces missing information, but is currently tested only
with programmed policies. Tang et al. analyze depth and width in
multi-agent systems [15]; their width denotes capability diversity,
whereas ours counts mutable registers. Our narrower candidate
contribution is an audited, stateful execution design that separates
retention, candidate selection, and feedback access, while measur-
ing how controller representation changes the resulting profile.
The current experiments validate that design with programmed
controllers; they do not establish the profile of a language model.
Diagnostic environments. The Behaviour Suite explicitly evalu-
atescorecapabilitiesthroughcarefullydesignedtasks[ 16].BabyAI
provides synthetic instruction-following levels of increasing diffi-
culty and a heuristic expert [17]. Our reference-policy design fol-
lows this diagnostic tradition. The workflow setting substitutes
dependency-constrained operations for movement and makes the
required work and failure rules directly inspectable. We have not
experimentally compared our instrument with either platform.
Interactive and long-horizon evaluation.𝜏-bench evaluates tool-
agent-user interaction using final database states [18]. Kwa et
al. measure the human-duration horizons of software tasks [19].
Those evaluation targets differ from a fixed-work intervention
on precedence. ARIES studies policies that transform interactive
thought graphs [20]; it does not provide evidence for indepen-
dence of the controls used here. The partial-observation literature
distinguishes current observations from history-conditioned in-
formation [21]. That distinction is central to our observation in-
tervention: omitted snapshots do not destroy information already
recoverable from history.
Tool-use benchmarks and nearest overlap.API-Bank, ToolBench,
and AppWorld evaluate tool-augmented models on runnable or
interactive tasks, while AgentBench spans multiple interactive en-
vironments [5–8]. MCPAgentBench is a particularly close recent
comparison: it evaluates multi-step tool invocation and presents
candidatetoollistswithdistractors[ 9].TRAJECT-Benchisacloser
overlapforcontrolledtrajectorystructureandintermediatefailure
analysis; RHB is a closer overlap for multi-step tool-use difficulty
and model-based experiments [10, 11]. MemCalib directly bench-
marks model use of retrieved memory, although it measures the
influence of natural-language propositions on responses rather
than retention of mutable workflow state [13]. Accordingly, nei-
ther multi-step tool use, trajectory depth, candidate selection, nor
memory evaluation is claimed as new here. Our candidate distinc-
tion is the fixed-work state-mutating intervention and its explicit
separation of visible snapshots from history-reconstructible state.
We have not reimplemented or directly compared against these
benchmark suites, and the programmed-controller and single-
model results cannot establish that this distinction improves eval-
uation of language models.
Reasoning-budget evaluation. Reasoning effort and inference
compute have already been studied as experimental variables, in-
cluding budget-matched comparisons of reasoning strategies [4].
The Illusion of Thinking also studies complexity-dependent rea-
soningeffort[ 2].Theopenempiricalquestionforthisprojectisnot
whether budget matters in general, but whether budget changes
model performance differently across audited state-retention,
candidate-selection, and feedback interventions. That interaction
has not been measured in this manuscript.

papers as methodological precedents, not as a systematic review
or an empirical comparison. Lipkis and Agogino study failure-
probability estimation and the difficulty of sampling rare out-
comes [22]. Our exact small-task probabilities serve a different
purpose: they expose when an empirical zero is a sampling lim-
itation. Dynamic Thinker optimizes decision-time search with

Depth 𝑑 ∈{1, 2, 4, 8}is the longest prerequisite chain, measured

Increasing depth changes precedence while preserving 𝑁 ,

and candidate levels, the same random draws are reused before
level-specific projections. This reduces avoidable surface variation
without asserting identical semantics across levels.

5 EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL
The full design has2 ×4 ×3 ×4 ×2 = 192 cells. Each uses seeds
0, . . . , 255 and all eight controllers, for 49,152 tasks and 393,216
episodes. The same seed is a paired block across levels and policies.
Repeated conditions are not treated as independent trials. The con-
figuration and analysis rules were written before the primary run
in a local protocol file; this is not an external preregistration.
The primary outcome is complete task success. We also record
completed operations, execution calls, candidate evaluations, and
first failure type. There are no exclusions. An Exact-controller fail-
ure aborts the run. Tests verify the full grid over additional seeds,
match graph schedule counts against exhaustive small-graph enu-
meration, check observation masking and public updates, and ex-
ercise explicit invalid-action behavior.
Within each family, a main contrast is the high-minus-low
endpoint success-rate difference, averaged equally over every
other level. We report all four contrasts and a specified width-by-
observation difference-in-differences:

with a fixed analysis seed. They are descriptive 95% bands, not si-

ondary outcomes are included in the supplementary artifact.

7.3 Snapshot cadence versus missing
information
Snapshot periods count successful operations, not tool attempts.
Period zero means the initial observation only; period one repeats
state after every success. With public deterministic updates, Ex-
act remains at 100% for periods zero, one, two, and four. Cache-2
reaches 0.29%, 100%, 4.64%, and 0.44%, respectively, on the𝑁 = 8
anchors. The difference is therefore a retention and refresh effect,
not a lack of history-conditioned information.
We then independently resample all active registers uniformly
after each successful operation. Feedback announces that resam-
pling occurred but does not reveal new values unless a snapshot is
due. Controllers invalidate stale cached values. Under initial-only
feedback, Exact now has zero observed successes, despite its per-
fecttrackinginthepublic-updatecondition.Withevery-stepsnap-
shots it returns to 100%; with period two it achieves 0.29%, and
period four produces zero observed successes.
This case has an information bound, not merely an algorithm-
specific failure rate. Without retries, let𝑣 = ⌈𝑁 /ℎ⌉be the number
of visible decisions at periodℎ > 0, and let𝑣 = 1 for initial-only
feedback. Any history-based controller has
𝑃 (complete)≤ 𝑏−(𝑁 −𝑣).
At each masked decision the newly resampled target code is uni-
form, independent of the entire visible history. An otherwise ex-
act controller attains the bound by selecting correctly at visible
steps and guessing at masked steps. For𝑁 = 8, initial-only feed-
back gives4−7 or 8−7, not literal impossibility. The aggregate ana-
lytic probability across the two candidate counts is approximately
0.0031%, explaining why a modest seed budget is unlikely to ob-
serve success.
7.4 Recovery changes the experiment
In the recovery grid, an invalid candidate returns its failure cate-
gory without changing state or marking an operation complete.
The episode ends only when its global error allowance𝑅 is ex-
ceeded. Successful operations still apply updates. The maximum
executionhorizonis 𝑁 +𝑅 calls.Wecompareindependentuniform
guesseswithcandidateelimination,whichremembersfailedcodes
for the current operation. This extra memory and failure feedback
must not be mistaken for additional internal reasoning.
With eight allowed errors and initial-only snapshots, uniform
elimination achieves 6.54% success versus 1.27% for replacement:
a paired gain of 5.27 points[4.15, 6.49]. Cache-2 with elimination

7.5 Locality and removal of state updates
Balanced and clustered query schedules have the same active
width and query counts. Clustering makes consecutive queries
use the same register along the deterministic ready schedule. At
𝑁 = 16 with public updates, Next-use-2 improves from 0.05% un-
der balanced queries to 1.86% under clustered queries: a paired
difference of 1.81 points[1.22, 2.44]. Reusing a confirmed residue
can therefore help, but the controller still fails most tasks. Fixed
Cache-2 has zero observed complete successes in both conditions;
its progress and call counts nevertheless differ. A completion-only
score misses that difference.
Concentratingeveryqueryonregisterzeroyields100%forboth
limited cache strategies. This is a deliberateinvalid width control:
the declared width remains four or eight, but the effective queried
widthisone.Wereportthatauditfailureinsteadofpresentingcon-
centration as a fair improvement at fixed memory demand.
The Stale-8 controller retains initial values but ignores updates.
Removing updates restores it from zero observed success on bal-
anced additive tasks to 100% on balanced static tasks; unrestricted
tracking remains at 100% in both. This negative control confirms
that the update mechanism matters. Static tasks are easier for this
stale strategy, not evidence that tracking mutable state is an intrin-
sic universal difficulty.

TheFixed-check-4 controllerevaluatesfourcandidatesperdecision

exactly the same action traces as Scan-1 under paired seeds, de-

Indexed constructs code-to-slot maps before execution and re-
tains all required state. It succeeds at 100% for all tested candi-
date counts, using eight execution lookups and𝑁𝑏 preparation
entries (Table6). Branching is therefore costly for the serial-scan
controller but not an inherent obstacle to this representation. In-
dex preparation grows with𝑏, and we do not compare total wall
time, energy, or token costs. The experiment is a counterexam-
pletorepresentation-freeinterpretationofabranchingprofile,not

7.7 Identifiers, floors, and aggregation
Reversing the randomly assigned operation identifiers preserves
the graph audit and Exact’s 100% success. It changes the determin-
istic ready order and hence some queried states. Scan-4’s paired
difference is−0.24 points [−0.59, 0.15]with initial-only feedback,
and Cache-2’s is−0.15 [−0.49, 0.15]. This limited relabeling check
does not establish invariance to arbitrary wording, prompts, or
tool names. Random scheduling remains too sparse at the selected
depth to resolve subtle differences.
Several ablations are dominated by ceiling and floor effects.
We retain zero-result conditions, exact probabilities where de-
rived, and secondary progress and resource outcomes rather than
regeneratetasksafterobservingsuccess.Thefollow-ongridiscon-
sequently useful for checking assumptions but not calibrated for
ranking unfamiliar agents. Aggregate percentages must always be
accompanied by the conditions averaged into them.
8 INTERPRETATION AND LIMITATIONS
The experiment shows that the instrument responds selectively to
limitations introduced by construction. It also supplies a concrete
counterexample to using depth alone as a universal difficulty mea-
sure: equal-depth, equal-work graphs can have different schedul-
ing probabilities. For an unfamiliar controller, these profiles would
be diagnostic evidence, not a unique identification of its internal
mechanism.
Several limitations constrain the claims. The tasks are small and
mechanical; the single execution tool omits natural-language in-
structions, tool discovery, and uncertain user goals. Immediate ter-
mination in the primary protocol magnifies compounding error
and is a design choice, not a typical feature of software workflows.
Even the follow-on maximum of sixteen operations does not rep-
resent long real-world workflows.
Active register count is not a lower bound on a controller’s re-
quired memory. A controller could retain values modulo𝑏, com-
press state, or precompute future answers, as the new controls
explicitly demonstrate. The restricted-cache results measure cho-
sen strategies, not minimum memory requirements. The hidden-
resampling extension is one extreme missing-information mecha-
nism, not a general model of partial observation, ambiguous feed-
back, or hidden user intent.
The graph families share the same state mechanics and are iden-
tical at the endpoint depths. The comparison is limited topology
replication, not independent-domain generalization. Main con-
trasts include documented couplings, and the equal-weight grid is
arbitrary. The large rollout count must not be confused with an
equally large number of independent samples. All endpoint inter-
actions are reported, but their bootstrap bands are descriptive and
can degenerate at rare-event floors. No fitted universal difficulty
model or out-of-domain predictive validation is supplied.
The next-use controller receives the same public graph as
other controllers, but exploits future query knowledge and learns
residues from successful trials. Its cache entry count does not in-
clude graph metadata, readiness bookkeeping, or controller code.
Likewise, the residue-bit counts cover stored values, not total

We ran a complete25 factorial of 256 tasks using the local, quan-

The protocol, factor grid, seeds, and analysis were fixed before this
planned run; the protocol is local rather than externally preregis-

The model received the complete public task specification, cur-
rent observation, and interaction history, and returned one JSON
action per decision. Invalid JSON or an invalid action ended the
episodewithoutaretry.Inferenceusedllama.cppserverb12030on

limit [27]. No hosted provider or paid inference was used. This is
a single, small-model evaluation, not a comparison of providers or
reasoning budgets. The separate runtime smoke test was excluded
from the factorial and is retained in the artifact.
Across the planned tasks, the model completed 0/256episodes
(0.000 success rate) and completed only 21 of 2048 required oper-

11 NEXT EMPIRICAL TEST
The next study should freeze the interaction harness and task pro-
tocol before inference; use larger paired samples; and compare
multiple models and providers. It should record model version,
every prompt and action, valid action rate, completed operations,
token usage, provider-reported cost, and latency. A reasoning-
budget intervention must use a supported, explicit budget control
and a total-usage-matched comparison. Retries and extra tool
calls must remain separate interventions. An independent state-
transition family and realistic transfer tasks are necessary before
claiming generality. The present account has a 24-episode pilot
and a 16-episode, two-seed fractional factorial using one Auto-
routed model, plus a local small-model run at the success floor.
Provider-level, cross-model, and reasoning-budget comparisons
remain uncompleted.
12 CONCLUSION
Structural task controls are useful only when their invariants
and couplings are explicit. Our fixed-work workflow instrument
distinguishes scheduling, candidate-search, and retention restric-
tions with executable controls and exact calibration targets. The
reference experiments show why a structural profile is more inter-
pretable than its aggregate score, while also showing why depth,
state width, and observation access cannot be treated as univer-
sally independent difficulty variables.
Thelocal-modelfactorialisafailedcalibrationofthemodel-task
interface, not evidence that the instrument yields a generalizable
profile: one quantized 0.5B model completed none of 256 work-
flows, creating a floor on the success endpoint. The completed-
operation and action-error records are useful diagnostics, but the
zeromarginalsuccesscontrastscannotbeinterpretedasstructural
invariance. A second, 16-episode Copilot study shows nonzero
contrasts,buttwotaskseedsandfractional-factorialaliasingmake
themunstabledescriptiveobservations,notevidenceofdimension-
specific effects. Claims about larger models, provider differences,
reasoning-budget effects, or transfer to real workflows remain
open.

balancedqueryassignment,updateregisters,nonzeroincrements,andcandidatepermutations.Candidate-countlevelsfilterthesameeight-

E WORKSHOP COMPARISON AND SUBMISSION SCOPE
We inspected four ALA 2025 papers as a purposive sample, not all workshop papers and not a systematic novelty search. Their total PDF
lengths are nine pages (failure sampling), ten (Dynamic Thinker), ten (task-graph scheduling), and eight (deliberation cost). References
begin on pages nine, eight, seven, and eight, respectively. Total PDF length therefore does not equal the allowed main-text length.
The historical ALA 2025 call allows original work up to eight pages excluding references, and preliminary or outlook papers up to
six pages. It specifies double-blind review and deanonymized camera-ready copies. Our expanded authored version is a preprint-style
manuscript with an appendix; the separatereview.tex source removes the author block and appendix, but must still be checked against
the current workshop’s track and page rules. A longer document is not evidence of acceptance or of a stronger contribution. No current-
edition acceptance, conference metadata, or submission date is asserted.
